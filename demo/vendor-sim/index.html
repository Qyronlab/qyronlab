How would an entangled pair run today?

Cloud quantum processors publish their calibration: how long each qubit keeps its state, how often a gate goes wrong, how often a readout is misread. This page takes only those published numbers and computes, in your browser, how good an entangled pair (a Bell pair) would come out on any coupled pair of qubits. Then it shows what happened when we actually ran such pairs — and whether the vendor's numbers had predicted it.

3. Did the vendor's numbers predict what we measured?

Before each run we sealed a prediction made from the vendor's numbers of that hour; after the run we compared. The readout ceiling needs no model at all: if the readout errors are independent, as the vendor's per-qubit numbers assume, a measured correlation cannot exceed it. When it does by more than three standard errors, the published readout numbers were not what the hardware did in our run.

4. What this is, and what it is not

ModelEach gate adds depolarizing noise equal to the vendor's published gate error; each readout is misread with the vendor's published probabilities, independently per qubit. Nothing is fitted to our data.
IgnoredCrosstalk, leakage, drift during the day, correlated readout errors. The model is deliberately the one the published numbers imply — so its misses show where the published numbers fall short.
